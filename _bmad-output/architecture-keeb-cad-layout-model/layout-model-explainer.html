keeb-cad · architecture · layout model v1

One tree. Six ideas. Every key accounted for.

This is how keeb-cad describes a keyboard. You drag keys, and the plate, PCB, case and firmware are recalculated from this one description. Nothing is drawn twice.

explicit over terseone way to do each thingsmall, coherent concepts
Idea 1 · tree

The tree says what belongs to what

A design is a tree. A side holds the matrix and the thumbs. The matrix holds columns, and columns hold slots. Moving a branch moves everything on it.

position = anchor frame + local offset

That's the only placement rule. Stagger is a column's offset. Splay is a column's rotation.


  


  

    

      Idea 2 · sequence and free
      
Things placed one after another

      
A sequence places each child after the previous one: one pitch along its direction, plus a gap, then the child's own offset and turn. A column is a sequence, a row of columns is a sequence, and a thumb fan is a sequence with turns.

      
Drag a key and the keys after it follow. Hold Alt and only that key moves, because the next key's gap absorbs the change.

      
step = pitch + gap → then offset (x, y, turn about pivot)

      
The other arrangement is free, where each child is placed on its own.

    

    

      
      

        
        
        
      

    

  


  

    

      Idea 3 · anchor
      
Owned by the side, positioned against a key

      
The thumb cluster belongs to the side, but it hangs off the bottom key of the inner column. Its anchor is that key, and it follows the key's full frame: position and rotation.

      
To keep the thumbs level, give them a cancelling turn. There's no "position only" switch.

      
anchor = reference node (by id) + attach point · full frame · no cycles

    

    

      
      

        
        
      

    

  


  

    

      Idea 4 · slot, part, pitch
      
Swap switches without redrawing

      
Every leaf is a slot holding one part: a switch cell, an encoder, an MCU, a screw, or nothing. Positions are written in u, and pitch decides how big a u is.

      
Switch type only sets the footprint and the minimum pitch. Corney Island uses Choc switches at 19 mm, so pitch has to be its own setting.

      
"1u" on x = pitch-x · "1u" on y = pitch-y

    

    

      
      

        
        
        
      

      

    

  


  

    

      Idea 5 · side mode
      
Reversible, or mirrored with overrides

      
Reversible: one PCB, flipped for the other hand. All three reference boards work this way. Only which parts are fitted can differ.

      
Mirrored: the right side is derived by mirroring the left across the centre line, then applying named overrides. Spacing and angle are just the left side's offset from the centre.

      
right = mirror(left) + overrides → ids "mirror:<left-id>"

    

    

      
      

        
        
        
      

    

  


  

    

      Idea 6 · the matrix
      
Wiring comes from the same tree

      
The PCB and the firmware must agree on which key sits on which row and column. So the model works it out once, and both read it.

      
nearest x-sequence → column · nearest y-sequence → row

      
The rule follows direction, not names, so column-staggered and row-staggered boards both work. You can override any key's row or column.

    Glossary
    
Each word means one thing

    

      
TermMeaning

      
        
designOne keyboard document. The only source of truth.

        
anchorThe node and attach point whose full frame (position and rotation) a node is positioned in.

        
offsetA node's own x, y and rotation, applied in its anchor's frame.

        
pivotThe point a rotation turns around. Used for nothing else.

        
sequenceChildren placed one after another by steps: pitch, gap, then offset.

        
freeChildren placed independently.

        
gapExtra distance before a child in a sequence.

        
slotA leaf with a size that holds one part.

        
partA switch cell, encoder, MCU module, battery, power switch, reset, screw, or empty.

        
switch cellYour key template: switch, diode, LED, stabiliser and per-key tracks.

        
pitchCentre-to-centre key distance (pitch-x, pitch-y). Independent of switch type.

        
side modeReversible (one geometry) or mirrored (copy plus overrides).

        
overrideA named change on the derived side: swap, remove, offset, populate.

        
matrixRow, column, diode direction, nets and MCU pins, worked out once by the model.

        
blockA reusable, versioned piece from the library, copied into your design with its origin and licence.
