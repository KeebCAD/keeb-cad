keeb-cad editor · Layout workspace · panel structure

Panel models at laptop size: docked, summoned, hybrid

Same neutral styling, same design, same selection in all three. Only the panel structure changes. Judge where the panels live, not the look.

Each screen is a 1366 × 768 laptop display at true 1:1 pixels. Chromium's own tab strip and toolbar take 72 px of that height, so the page gets 1366 × 696. Fixed in every variant: the top bar with the five workspace tabs (Ctrl+1..5) and their status badges, the bottom status strip (24 px), thumb-home selected, the overlap violation between thumb-home and thumb-tucky, and group frames with drag handles (click a key to select the slot, grab a frame handle to select the group). With both bars, 632 px of height is left for panels and canvas.

Canvas area = the canvas rectangle in px, divided by the whole 1366 × 768 screen. Zoom = px per mm needed to fit the left side (174 × 116 mm view), relative to true 1:1 scale (3.78 px/mm at 96 dpi).

Gains

  • Nothing is hidden. Tree, parameter values and inspector are always visible, with no keystroke to remember and no state to recall.
  • The tree also selects things the canvas cannot reach easily: groups, collapsed columns, parts, and parameters next to the slots that use them.

Costs

  • Smallest canvas. On a 1366-wide screen the canvas is limited by width, so the keyboard draws at 128% against 144% in (b) and (c), 11% smaller linearly.
  • The tree is idle during most drag-and-type edits, yet it permanently takes 240 px. Getting that space back means collapsing it by hand and reopening it later.

Gains

  • Largest canvas. The inspector sits next to thumb-home, so the eye moves very little between dragging and correcting the number.
  • Everything else is a single key (T, P) or Ctrl+K away, which suits an expert who knows the glossary names.

Costs

  • The floating inspector covers canvas next to the selection and must move when the selection moves. It can sit on top of the neighbour you want to click next.
  • At 16:9 the height is the limit, so full bleed gives the same 144% zoom as (c). The extra width becomes empty margin, and the tree and parameter values are out of sight until summoned.

Gains

  • The inspector, the panel used on every edit, always stays in the same place and never covers the canvas.
  • Same 144% keyboard zoom as (b), because height is the limit at this size. The docked inspector costs no drawing scale, only side margin.

  • Two panel behaviours to learn: the inspector is docked and the tree slides out. Collapsing the inspector (›) has to stay a manual toggle.
